Where a board goes

A board an agent creates, or pulls onto the stage, lands to the right of what is on the canvas — top-aligned with it, rather than below the deck. A position the agent gives is used as it stands. Superseded in one respect: this was written when “placed” meant a position in deck.json, which is one arrangement for everybody — see the canvas belongs to the conversation for the correction and the rule as it shipped.

Problem

The default was rows of three, below everything, anchored at the left edge of the whole arrangement. On a deck that has grown into regions that is nowhere in particular: this deck has 510 boards spread over 750,000 pixels vertically, so a board written in the middle of a conversation appeared at the far end of whichever column happened to be lowest, and the person who asked for it had to go and find it.

Method

The three rules

  • The reference is what is on the canvas — the boards in play, not the whole deck. A deck's arrangement is every region at once, which is to say no place in particular.
  • A board this stage has placed is never moved. The stage's own record holds the positions it decided — not deck.json, which is the deck's arrangement and everybody's default. That correction is what made this rule simpler rather than more complicated, and it is why the call is safe on every show.
  • Nothing lands on top of anything. A spot that collides is walked downwards until it is clear, because a board put beside the ones in play can still land on a board nobody is looking at.

Where it lives

deck/layout.ts is the arithmetic, pure and 7 tests of its own. Two callers: Deck.autoPlace for a deck being opened — where nothing is in play yet, so the arranged boards are the reference, and a deck with nothing arranged lays out from the origin exactly as before — and StageService.place, which writes the position down and broadcasts it.

newBoard, attach, show and mirror all call it. Placement happens before the camera is told to fit, and both travel the same socket in order, so the browser frames where the board now is rather than where it was.

A position the agent names

stage.newBoard({ …, at: { x, y } }) is used as it stands — a caller that has thought about where something goes is not improved on. It is in stage.d.ts, so it is part of the contract agents read. Otherwise the default applies, and stage.move still works afterwards for anything else.
