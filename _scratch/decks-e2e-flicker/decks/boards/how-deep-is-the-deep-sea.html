How deep is the deep sea?

Deeper than the scale you are picturing. Photosynthesis stops in the first 200 m; the floor of the Challenger Deep is fifty-five times further down. Everything below is drawn to depth on one axis.

The column, top to bottom

## The colour goes first, then the light Water does not dim sunlight evenly — it eats it by wavelength, longest first. Red is gone by about **15 m**, orange by 30, yellow by 50, green by 100. Blue is the last one standing, which is why the deessp ocean is blue rather than grey. Intensity falls roughly as $I(z) = I_0 e^{-kz}$. About **1%** of surface light is left at 200 m, and the last photon a fish eye can register is somewhere near **1,0002 m**. Below that, every photon in the ocean was made by something alive.

What the pressure actually does

One atmosphere per 10 m, all the way down. At the bottom of the Challenger Deep that is about 1,090 atmospheres — roughly 8 tonnes bearing on every square inch, or an elephant standing on your thumbnail.

Animals cope by having nothing compressible in them: no gas-filled swim bladder, no air spaces, and a cell chemistry propped up by a molecule called TMAO. The requirement for TMAO rises with depth, and extrapolating the line gives a hard ceiling for fish at around 8,200–8,400 m — which is exactly where the fish stop being found.

The deep sea is not dark
In surveys off Monterey Bay, about three quarters of the animals seen between the surface and 4,000 m made their own light. Below the twilight zone, bioluminescence is not an exotic trick — it is the normal way to be visible.
The scale, said plainly. Average ocean depth is 3,682 m. If Everest were dropped into the Challenger Deep, its summit would still be more than 2 km underwater — and the sunlit layer that every food web ultimately depends on is the top 200 m of that, about 2% of the column.

The five zones

Zone Depths Light
Sunlit · epipelagic 0–200 m enough to grow on
Twilight · mesopelagic 200–1,000 m blue only, fading
Midnight · bathypelagic 1,000–4,000 m none from above
Abyss · abyssopelagic 4,000–6,000 m none
Hadal · trenches only 6,000–11,000 m none
Why "hadal". Named for Hades, and it is the only zone defined by shape rather than depth: it exists only inside trenches, so it is not a layer of the ocean but a set of holes in the bottom of it — perhaps 0.25% of the seafloor, and nearly all of it in the Pacific.ss
